Use case · BESS in southern Moldova

Can a battery work in the south?

The south is where the permits are. It is also where many connections run through 110 kV lines that start at MGRES (the Cuciurgan plant in the Transnistrian region) or end in Bender. MGRES has not supplied the right bank since 1 January 2025. The figures below come from the permit register, edition 14 Aug 2026. "South" here means Ștefan Vodă, Căușeni, Cimișlia, Leova and Ceadîr-Lunga, the southern districts with permits. Cahul, Vulcănești, Comrat, Taraclia, Basarabeasca and Cantemir have none.

  • The development plan calls overloads on the 400 kV Vulcănești–Isaccea and 110 kV Vulcănești–Bolgrad lines under N-1 its "most serious problem". A battery here could be offered as congestion relief, but expect operating limits.

  • The largest renewable cluster in the country, and wind needs storage here.
  • Wind-auction winners Leafwind and Navitas hold permits here and must add at least 0.25 MWh per MW. They could be partners or competitors.
  • Most connections are taps on 110 kV lines from MGRES. If those lines can't carry power, a battery is stranded with them.

Next step: before any revenue case, get Moldelectrica's view on how secure the MGRES–Cioburciu, –Caplani and –Purcari lines are, and on the timing of the Ștefan Vodă 400 kV substation. A battery could be timed to it.

Feasibility in one paragraph

The need is real. The south holds 368 MW of wind and solar with no storage, the system has an evening deficit of over 600 MW, and the Energy Community's study sizes 162 MW of upward reserve for about 1,261 MW of variable renewables. But arbitrage alone is a weak base for a battery. At about €1M per MW for 2 hours (the study's capex), the seasonal ranges give €38k–103k per MW per year, a simple payback of about 10–27 years. The realised Romanian prices above (Oct–Nov 2026) sit inside that range, but they cover one season, not a year. A southern battery works when it also earns balancing payments or is paid for by a wind project's storage obligation, and when it connects at Vulcănești or on a right-bank line. MGRES-dependent connections in Ștefan Vodă need clearing with Moldelectrica first.

What this scan cannot see yet

Distribution permits are missingThe register covers permits issued by Moldelectrica only. Premier Energy Distribution had issued 329 MW and RED Nord 96.4 MW (Jul 2025), with no breakdown by location. Ministry of Energy
A permit is not a projectMoldelectrica had issued about 1,500 MW of permits by Feb 2025, and under 10% get built each year. Read high scores as pressure, not certainty. Ministry of Energy
Romanian prices, not MoldovanThe daily spread uses Romanian day-ahead prices, the market Moldova imports from. OPEM's Moldovan prices could not be reached from here. Energy-Charts' data is for private and internal use only.
No hosting capacity by nodeUnlike Denmark, there is no public free/limited/no-capacity map. Closeness to the backbone is a stand-in for grid strength, measured in straight-line km from the district seat.
The Transnistrian flag is a text ruleA permit is flagged when its connection point names MGRES, CERS Moldovenească, Bender or Rîbnița. It does not say whether the line works today. Only Moldelectrica can say that.
Positions are approximateEach district is drawn at its seat town. Nodes in the Transnistrian region (MGRES, Rîbnița) are shown but left out of the closeness score.
Storage figures disagreeOne source gives 150 MW installed (Jun 2026). Another gives about 30 systems totalling about 150 MWh (Aug 2026). Both are shown as published. InfoMarket
